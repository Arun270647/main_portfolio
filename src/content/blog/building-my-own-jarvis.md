Quick question: if your laptop could talk back, what would you ask it first?

I've wanted an answer to that since the first time I watched Iron Man. 🦾

## The dream

Tony Stark doesn't click through folders. He just talks. *"JARVIS, pull up the files."* Done.

On 10 October I decided to stop wishing and start building my own. An assistant on my laptop that I can talk to, and that actually *does* things: opens apps, finds files, starts my projects.

## Version one: clunky but alive

The first version worked, sort of.

It waited for a wake word before listening, like the smart speakers do. When it answered, it showed a wall of text. It felt less like JARVIS and more like a very polite command prompt.

## Then it started freezing 🥶

Every now and then the whole thing would just stop. No answer, no error, nothing.

I assumed the AI was the problem. It wasn't.

Two parts of my assistant were fighting over the same small slice of my laptop's graphics memory. Think of two people trying to sit on the same chair. Neither of them gets to sit, and everyone waits.

Once I gave each one its own space, the freezing stopped.

## The turn: taking things away

Here's what surprised me. The biggest upgrades came from *removing* things.

- **No more wake word.** Instead, a glowing orb appears on my screen every time my laptop starts, and a shortcut key wakes it up.
- **No more wall of text.** Short answers, and actions instead of explanations.

![The glowing orb sitting above my taskbar](/blog/building-my-own-jarvis/orb-on-desktop.jpg)

And because every assistant needs a personality: every time I open it, it greets me with a new line written just for me that day.

## Today

Tony has his JARVIS. Now I have mine.

It can open a terminal and start my coding tools by itself. I talk, it builds the setup, and I start working.

![My assistant waiting for a command](/blog/building-my-own-jarvis/awaiting-command.jpg)

> The best feature I added was removing the wake word.

## What I took away

1. **When something breaks, the obvious suspect is often innocent.** It wasn't the AI. It was two parts fighting over memory.
2. **Less friction beats more features.** One key press beats saying a magic word out loud fifty times a day.
3. **Personality is a feature.** A new greeting every morning makes me smile every single time.
