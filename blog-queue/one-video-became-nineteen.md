Quick question: what's a boring task you finally automated?

Mine involved nineteen screen recordings and zero patience. 🎥

## The project

During my internship at Gradtwin, I built a research dashboard for scientists working on aluminium recovery from bauxite.

Here's what it does in plain words. Scientists run experiments under different conditions: temperature, time, amounts of each ingredient. Each experiment takes time and money. My dashboard lets them type in the conditions, and trained AI models *predict* the result before they run anything.

Think of it like a weather forecast, but for a lab experiment.

![The dashboard predicting a recovery result (demo data)](/blog/one-video-became-nineteen/prediction.jpg)

## Three helpers at once 🧠

The project was big, so I tried something new. I ran three AI coding sessions at the same time, like a small team.

One acted as the "brain": it planned the work and handed tasks to the other two. The other two built.

I redesigned the dashboard from a design handoff, connected the real prediction models and put it online, using free hosting only.

Then I submitted it. Done, I thought.

## The twist

My mentor replied with one request: *"Can you send a video of every module?"*

Every module. There were **nineteen** of them.

Recording nineteen screens by hand means nineteen times of clicking through the same steps, making a mistake on step six and starting again. A full afternoon, gone.

## The turn

So instead of recording nineteen videos, I wrote one small script.

It opens the dashboard, walks through each module exactly like a person would, and records the screen as it goes. Run it once, and nineteen videos appear.

No typos. No retakes. No afternoon lost.

![One frame from every module video the script recorded](/blog/one-video-became-nineteen/all-modules.jpg)

> If you'll do something 19 times, teach a computer to do it once.

## What I took away

1. **Repetition is a signal.** The moment you're doing something a third time, ask if a computer could do it.
2. **AI helpers work better as a team with a lead**, just like people do.
3. **Free hosting is enough to prove an idea.** You don't need a big budget to ship something real.
