Quick question: do you remember your first interview? The nerves, the waiting, the "did I say something stupid?" replay in your head?

Mine wasn't for a job. It was for an internship. And it almost didn't happen. 👀

## The link

It started the way most good things do: by accident.

A friend sent me a link to an internship at Gradtwin. No big plan, no preparation, no "this is my dream company". I just applied, randomly.

Then they called me in. Boom.

## Round 1: the aptitude test 🧮

First came an aptitude test. Numbers, logic, patterns, a timer ticking in the corner.

It's a strange feeling. You build things every day, and suddenly you're solving puzzles that have nothing to do with what you build. But that's the point: it's not testing what you know, it's testing how you think.

## Round 2: the group discussion 🗣️

Next, a group discussion. The topic: **social media, good or bad?**

I stood for *good*.

A group discussion isn't about winning an argument. It's about being heard without talking over people, and making your point in the few seconds you get before someone else jumps in. Harder than any code I've written.

## The wait ⏳

This is the part I'll never forget.

After the GD, we waited. And waited. **Three to four hours** before anyone told us who was going through to the HR interview.

Every minute, my confidence dropped a little. By the end, I had nearly convinced myself: *that's it, I'm not even getting to the HR round.* I was already planning what to tell my friend.

Then they read out the names. Mine was on the list.

## Round 3: the HR interview

After four hours of convincing myself I'd failed, the HR interview felt almost easy. I just talked, honestly.

And right after it, they offered me the internship. Offer letter in hand, the same day.

> The waiting room is the hardest round. Nobody prepares you for it.

## What the internship actually taught me

At Gradtwin, I built a web platform for scientists working on aluminium recovery from bauxite: they enter a material's details and get a clearly labelled estimate, and save scenarios to compare later.

![Analysing a material sample on the platform (demo data)](/blog/first-interview-for-an-internship/analysis.jpg)

But the bigger lesson wasn't the platform. It was learning to **think and work like a real company**:

- Requirements written down *before* any code
- A design handed to me that I had to build faithfully, not "my way"
- Every account approved by an admin, because real data needs real gatekeeping
- Honest labels everywhere: an estimate is called an estimate, never a promise
- Deadlines that someone else is counting on

![A result screen, labelled clearly as an estimate (demo data)](/blog/first-interview-for-an-internship/estimate.jpg)

## What I took away

1. **Apply anyway.** I almost didn't. The "random" application changed my year.
2. **The wait isn't a verdict.** Four hours of silence meant nothing about the result.
3. **An internship is a rehearsal for real work.** Treat it like the real thing and it becomes the real thing.
