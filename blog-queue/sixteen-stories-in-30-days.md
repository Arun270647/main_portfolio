Quick question: have you ever tried posting something every two days for a month?

I just did. Sixteen stories, thirty days. 📅

## Why I did it

Most of what I build is invisible. A page that loads in 0.6 seconds instead of 53. A work tracker that ends the 7 PM "any updates?" messages. A robot that checks my website every week.

Nobody sees that work unless you tell the story. So for a month, I told one every two days.

## What telling them taught me

**1. The story is never the code.** Every post started with a person: a coach on a ground at 6 AM, a student who forgot a password, a teammate asking "who's on this?". The code was just how the story ended.

**2. The twist is the best part.** Nobody remembers "I built a feature". They remember "it took 53 seconds", "it was 718 MB", "they never got the email". The thing that went wrong is what makes a story worth reading.

**3. Deleting is a skill.** Again and again, the turn in the story was *removing* something: the wake word, the sign-up page, the passwords, half a course. I didn't expect that pattern when I started writing. It was in almost every post.

**4. Writing it down makes you a better builder.** Explaining a fix to someone who's never written code forces you to understand *why* it worked, not just that it did.

> Building is half the job. Telling the story is the other half.
