Quick question: have you ever built something "just to try it"… and then people started using it for real?

That's how Track My Academy grew up. 👀

## The problem on the ground

Walk into most sports academies and you'll find the same three tools: a paper register, a WhatsApp group and an Excel sheet that only one person understands.

Attendance on paper. Fees in a notebook. Progress? "Coach remembers."

## Day one: one prompt ⚡

In July 2025 the first version of Track My Academy was generated by an AI app builder. One prompt, one working demo.

It looked like a real product. You could click around, add players and see a dashboard. For a demo, it was magic.

## Then real academies showed up

A demo only has to work once, in front of you. A product has to work every morning at 6 AM, for a coach standing on a ground with a whistle in one hand and a phone in the other.

So we kept building:

- **Attendance**, made faster with every round of coach feedback
- **Performance tracking built sport by sport**, because a cricket coach and a football coach don't measure progress the same way
- **Fees and receipts**, so nobody has to chase payments in a notebook
- **Certificates and ID cards**, generated in a click
- **A mobile app**, plus separate logins for coaches and parents

## The scary part 😬

At one point the engine underneath needed a full rebuild. For a short while, 29 features broke at the same time.

Imagine renovating a house while the family is still living in it. You fix the wiring room by room, and you check every light switch before anyone notices.

It held together. And it taught me that the work nobody sees is what makes the product trustworthy.

## Today

More than 3,000 changes later, Track My Academy runs for a network of around 50 academies and 2,000 players.

My role grew with it too: from building the website, to managing the product, to leading the technology as CTO.

> AI can get you to a demo in a day. Getting to a product academies trust takes everything after.
