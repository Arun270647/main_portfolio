Quick question before we start: how does attendance work where you are? Paper register? Excel sheet? Someone shouting names across a ground at 6 AM?

Hold that thought. 👀

## The message that started it

One day a client reached out with a problem that sounded simple:

> "We're tracking attendance and performance manually. It's really hard to keep track. Can you build us a custom feature that actually makes our work easier?"

Manual meant pen, paper and a lot of hope. They wanted something that would make the coaches' lives easier.

Challenge accepted.

## Idea #1: Swipe right if they showed up 🔥

My first idea? Tinder.

Hear me out. Every player shows up as a card. Swipe right, present. Swipe left, absent. Everyone already knows how to swipe. Zero training needed.

And honestly? It worked well at first. It felt fun, and coaches got it instantly.

But as time went on, swiping through a whole batch, one card at a time, every single day, got hard to manage.

## Idea #2: Assume the best ✅

So we flipped it.

Instead of asking "who's here?", we asked "who's missing?"

Now every student is marked present by default. The coach just taps the few who didn't show up. Most days, most kids turn up, so it's a couple of taps instead of a whole deck of swipes.

Boring? Yes. Better? Massively.

**The lesson:** the coolest UI isn't always the best UX. The best design is the one that saves your user the most taps.

## Performance tracking, built sport by sport 🏏⚽

Attendance tells you who showed up. Performance tells you whether showing up is actually working.

Here's the thing: a football coach and a cricket coach don't measure progress the same way. So instead of one generic form for everyone, we built performance tracking specifically for each sport and put it right into the platform.

Coaches assess players on what actually matters in their sport, and the progress is right there on the website whenever they need it.

Today, Track My Academy runs this for a network of around 50 academies and 2,000 players.

## What I took away

1. **Find the action users repeat most, and make it cheaper.** Attendance happens every single day, so every saved tap counts.
2. **Fun ideas make great prototypes, not always great products.** The Tinder version wasn't a failure; it got us to the better idea faster.
3. **One size fits none.** Build for how each sport actually works, not for a generic "player".
