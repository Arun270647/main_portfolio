Quick question: if a stranger fact-checked every line on your profile, how many would survive?

Mine didn't all survive. Here's how I found out. 👀

## The problem with a developer's portfolio

When you build websites for a living, your own website is the one people judge hardest. A plumber's house can have a leaky tap. A developer's portfolio can't have a single broken button.

So in one week of October I decided mine needed a fresh look.

![My portfolio before the rebuilds: the old terminal-style design](/blog/rebuilt-my-portfolio-three-times/before.jpg)

## Rebuild #1: a brand-new design 🎨

I started from scratch with a whole new design. I built it, ran it once, looked at it on my screen…

…and threw it away.

It was pretty, but it didn't feel like *me*. It could have been anyone's portfolio.

## Rebuild #2: one long scroll 📜

Next idea: put everything on one long page. Scroll from top to bottom and you've seen my whole story.

On paper that's simple. In practice it felt like reading a phone book. Nobody wants to scroll past twenty projects to find the one they came for.

So I switched back.

## Rebuild #3: the one that stuck ✅

The third version kept the parts that felt like me:

- A section called **"From my workspace to the Internet"** with a photo of my actual desk: *3 monitors. 12 terminals. Infinite projects.*
- My career told as **"The Sacred Timeline"**, because every Marvel fan knows a timeline is more fun than a list.

![The Sacred Timeline section of my portfolio](/blog/rebuilt-my-portfolio-three-times/sacred-timeline.jpg)

That's when I thought I was done.

## The hard edit ✂️

Then I looked at my projects with a stranger's eyes.

Two of my best-looking projects weren't really *mine*. I built them as part of my job, for the companies I worked for. Showing them as my own personal projects wasn't honest. So they moved to my work experience, where they belong.

And one line I loved, "Intern to CTO in 18 months", came out too. It was true. But it sounded unbelievable, and a line people don't believe hurts you more than it helps.

> A portfolio isn't what you've done. It's what you can honestly claim.

## What I took away

1. **Throwing a version away isn't wasted time.** Each rebuild told me what I *didn't* want.
2. **Personality beats polish.** My real desk photo does more than any fancy animation.
3. **Credibility is a design decision.** What you cut matters as much as what you add.
