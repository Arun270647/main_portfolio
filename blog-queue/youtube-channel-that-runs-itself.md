Quick question: would you watch a YouTube channel where AI makes every single video?

Be honest. I wasn't sure either. 🎬

## The first idea

My first plan was simple: take movies, cut them into short clips and post them as Shorts.

Then I asked myself a better question. Why cut up someone else's story when AI could tell a new one?

Could AI **write a story, voice it and animate it**, all while I sleep?

## The first video 🤖

I built it. One part writes the script, another voices it, another turns it into a video.

The first video came out and it was technically perfect. Clear voice, no mistakes, right length.

And it was completely forgettable.

The script was generic, the kind of "believe in yourself" story you've heard a hundred times. The visuals looked like a slideshow. Nobody would stop scrolling for it. I barely watched it to the end myself.

## The turn: give it a soul

So I stopped fixing the machine and started fixing the *story*.

- **I picked the voice by ear.** I tested several voices against each other until one actually felt right. No settings page tells you that. You have to listen.
- **I tuned the stories for a South Indian audience.** Familiar places, familiar feelings, familiar humour.
- **I switched to motion graphics**, so the video actually moves instead of flipping through pictures.
- **I deleted the automation tool I'd started with.** It turned out I didn't need it, and fewer moving parts means fewer things break at 6 AM.

![A frame from a motivational Short made by the pipeline](/blog/youtube-channel-that-runs-itself/frame-motivational.jpg)

## Today ☀️

Every morning at 6 AM, one AI comes up with a story idea: motivational, horror, funny or mystery. Another writes it. Another animates it.

Nobody has to wake me up. By the time I do, there's a new video ready.

![A frame from a horror Short made by the pipeline](/blog/youtube-channel-that-runs-itself/frame-horror.jpg)

The channel isn't public yet. Every finished video waits for one tap from me before it goes out, because the last 1% (*is this actually good?*) is still my job.

> The first AI video was perfect, and that was the problem. It had no story.

## What I took away

1. **Perfect isn't the same as good.** No mistakes doesn't mean anybody cares.
2. **Taste is the part you can't automate.** Choosing the voice was the most human part of the whole project.
3. **Delete what you don't need.** The tool I removed made the project better.
