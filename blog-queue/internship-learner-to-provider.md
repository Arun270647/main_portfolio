Quick question: what's one thing your first internship got wrong, or got right?

I've sat on both sides of that table now. Here's what I learned from each. 👀

## When I was the intern

In 2024 I did my first internships at Novitech: web development first, then AI and machine learning research. In early 2025 came Android development at Corizo.

![My internship certificates from Novitech and Corizo](/blog/internship-learner-to-provider/then-certificates.jpg)

I learned a lot. I'm grateful for all of it.

But I also learned what being an intern *feels* like:

- Waiting for someone to give you a task
- Sitting through slides that went over your head, nodding anyway
- Getting a certificate at the end and wondering what you actually built

## Two years later: the other side of the table

Today I'm the CTO at TrackMy Tech. I have interns on my own team.

I built the internship platform that 140+ students applied through. And I teach a course to beginners who had never heard the word HTML.

Suddenly *I* was the one deciding how an internship feels.

## Fixing what I sat through 🔧

So I made every choice by remembering what I wished someone had done for me:

- **Real work from week one.** Nobody should wait days for their first task.
- **Pictures before words.** If a slide goes over a beginner's head, the slide is the problem, not the beginner.
- **Classes that unlock only when we're live,** so nobody runs ahead and nobody falls behind.
- **Homework that gets checked quickly,** because feedback two weeks late is just a grade.

![Now: the mentor board for the internship I run](/blog/internship-learner-to-provider/now-mentor-board.jpg)

## The plot twist

Here's the part I like most: I'm *still* an intern.

In 2026, while working as a CTO, I also did an internship at Gradtwin, building a research dashboard for scientists. (That story is coming next week.)

Being on both sides at the same time keeps me honest. I still know exactly how it feels to wait for a reply from a mentor.

> The best internship I can give is the one I wish I'd had.

## What I took away

1. **Remember how it felt.** It's the best design guide for anything you build for beginners.
2. **Certificates are proof you attended. Projects are proof you learned.**
3. **You never stop being the learner.** The title changes. The curiosity shouldn't.
