Quick question: has someone ever believed in you before you believed in yourself?

It happened to me at 7 PM on an ordinary evening. 📩

## The message

My phone buzzed. A message from my professor, with the brochure for our department conference attached.

The message said I'd been put forward. My name was already in. *"Just do a good paper and present it well."*

That was it.

No topic. No format. Nothing about what kind of paper, or even much about the conference itself. Just one more line at the end:

> *"I know you can do it. As usual, you rock."*

## High stakes

I read that line more than once.

It wasn't pressure exactly. It was something bigger: **someone had put their trust in me before I'd done anything to earn it this time.** They'd given my name without asking what I'd write.

So my mind went one way only: *I need to win this.*

## From nothing to an idea

The first problem was obvious. I had a conference. I had no idea.

So I made a plan, and I read. A lot. Research papers, articles, documents, anything that could tell me two things:

- **What does a good research paper actually look like?**
- **What problem is worth writing about?**

Slowly, one problem kept pulling me back. The more I read about it, the more sure I was: this is the one.

That problem became my paper: **SlopGuard**. The idea behind it deserves its own post, so I'll tell that story soon. 👀

All of it started with one message and one line of belief.

> Sometimes the only instruction you get is someone believing in you. Follow that one.

## What I took away

1. **Belief is an instruction.** "I know you can do it" told me more than any format guide could.
2. **No brief? Start reading.** When nobody tells you what to do, research is how you find out.
3. **Say yes, then work like it matters.** The trust was the start. The work had to earn it.
