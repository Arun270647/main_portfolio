Quick question: have you ever said yes to something, then had to Google what you'd said yes to?

I have. And it turned into one of my favourite projects. 🛍️

## The message

I was still early in building websites when a client reached out. They were starting a premium clothing and accessories brand, Kyra Elite, and they wanted an e-commerce website.

I said yes.

Here's the confession: **I didn't know what e-commerce meant.** Not "I didn't know how to build it". I didn't know what the *word* meant.

## Homework first

So before writing a single line of code, I went back to school, on my own.

E-commerce, it turns out, just means **selling things online**. A shop, but on the internet. And a shop isn't only the part customers see. It has:

- **A shop window:** the pages where people browse products.
- **A cart and a till:** where they pick things and pay.
- **A back room:** where the owner adds new stock, changes prices and checks orders.

Once I saw it as a shop, everything made sense. I learned how the pieces fit together, and only when I had the basics did I start building.

![The Kyra Elite home page: "Wear Your Light"](/blog/first-ecommerce-client/cover.jpg)

## The back room matters most

The customers see the shop window. But the owner *lives* in the back room.

So the store came with its own dashboard, where the Kyra team can add products and manage the shop themselves without calling me every time they get new stock.

I delivered it ahead of schedule. Karthick, Kyra's product owner, later wrote:

> "The admin dashboard he built has streamlined our entire workflow."

![The Kyra Elite brand line: "Elegance is not about being noticed, it's about being remembered."](/blog/first-ecommerce-client/remembered.jpg)

## Today

Kyra Elite is live at [kyraelite.com](https://kyraelite.com). It started with me not knowing a word, and ended with a real brand's online store.

> You don't need to know everything to say yes. You need to be willing to learn it before you start.

## What I took away

1. **Not knowing is a starting point, not a stop sign.** Everybody starts by looking things up.
2. **Learn the idea before the code.** "It's a shop" taught me more than any tutorial.
3. **Build for the person who uses it every day.** For a store, that's the owner as much as the customer.
