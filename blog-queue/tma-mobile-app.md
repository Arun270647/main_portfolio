Quick question: what's the one app you'd delete tomorrow if it crashed one more time?

Everyone has one. My job was making sure Track My Academy never became that app. 📱

## Coaches don't sit at desks

A coach's office is a ground at 6 AM. Sun in their eyes, kids everywhere, no laptop in sight.

So if Track My Academy only worked well on a computer, it didn't really work for coaches. It had to live on their phones.

![The coach home screen at 6 AM (demo data)](/blog/tma-mobile-app/coach-home.jpg)

## Building the app

We turned Track My Academy into a proper phone app:

- **Sign in with Google**, so there's no password to remember
- **Links that open straight in the app**, instead of a browser tab
- **Notifications**, so coaches and parents hear about what matters without checking

![Marking attendance on the phone (demo data)](/blog/tma-mobile-app/attendance.jpg)

## The twist: a hundred ways to break 😬

A phone app has a hundred small ways to go wrong. A button that's hidden on a small screen. A page that looks fine on one phone and broken on another. A form that loses everything if a call comes in halfway.

So in October I did a full health check of the app, screen by screen.

It found **30 real problems.**

## The fix, and the fix of the fix

We fixed all 30 in one sprint. Then the report came back: "All fixed."

I didn't fully believe it. So I had every fix checked a second time, by someone (and something) that hadn't written it.

And sure enough, "all fixed" wasn't completely true. A few fixes had only been half done.

It's like marking your own exam paper. You'll always read what you *meant* to write, not what you actually wrote.

Only after that second check, plus testing every screen by hand, did the app go live.

> Never let the one who wrote the fix be the one who says it's fixed.

## Today

Today coaches take attendance, check their batch and see who still owes fees, all from the phone in their pocket, standing right there on the ground.

## What I took away

1. **Build for where your users actually are.** For coaches, that's a ground, not a desk.
2. **Health checks find what feedback doesn't.** Users rarely report small bugs. They just quietly stop using the app.
3. **Always get a second pair of eyes.** Especially on the fixes you're proudest of.
